Remittance

We carry Burmese workers' money home.

Makes money

A fee and the exchange spread on each transfer, through Laila's counter next door and our own payment system.

Byproduct

Money keeps moving when banks shut or a border closes. Money is how people get out.

Mood

Today it moves by hundi: cash handed over in Mae Sai, paid out by a broker in Tachileik, settled by phone. Older than any bank.

Shareholder value: maximized. Merit: accrued.

Byproduct

How the byproduct works

Everyday conveniences that double as an escape kit.

People use the map to find a pump repair, the remittance to pay a mother's rent, the papers to open a shop. Every use rehearses the way out: the route, the money, the documents. The day a government turns on its people, the kit is already in their pocket and already works.

Mood

Every year the Akha rebuild the gate at the village edge, guardians carved beside it. Nobody calls it a security budget.

Shareholder value: maximized. Merit: accrued.

Bot billing

We charge the bots that read us.

Makes money

AI agents and crawlers already read motdang every night. They pay per call, per answer, per booking, automatically.

Byproduct

The machines pay, so the people using the map, the papers and the remittance can pay less.

Mood

The giants Pu Sae and Ya Sae guard Chiang Mai for a buffalo a year. The city still pays. The oldest subscription in Lanna.

Shareholder value: maximized. Merit: accrued.

Merit ledger

We count merit (บุญ) in baht, next to revenue.

Each deal records the merit it made: temples, wells, schools, rice for the monks at dawn. Thai customers buy from a company whose merit they can see. Every account closes on two lines.
